How I run a revenue engine

Where it came from, what it is for, and one caveat.

Where it came from

Phil, the COO at Black & White Zebra, introduced drivers and levers at a leadership session, and it stuck with me. Business had always come naturally to me, the way customer service, sales, operations and the books fit together, but this connected the story to the data. I used it to put the conversion map I had already built, traffic to revenue, onto one model, and ran a tornado analysis to see which levers were worth the quarter. Then I noticed it applied at every business I worked with after that.

What it is for

Everything in a business contributes to revenue, not just sales. This is how I hold all of it at once, in a form you can point at, analyze and argue about. It takes the subjectivity out of setting quarterly priorities: a project gets attention when it moves a line that rolls up to the number, and every line has an owner, a cadence and a source of truth. I am not saying a company should adopt this. It is the way my brain works, and it is what makes business make sense to me. It is also, I think, the view a head of revenue has to be able to hold.

One caveat

I have not owned every piece of a tree like this at a single company. I have owned new revenue end to end, run retention across a territory, and built the forecast and cadence over the top. The tree on screen is a typical B2B software business, not a rule; every business cuts differently, and finding the cut that is true for yours is the first job. The numbers are an illustrative scenario, and the four case pages say which parts are proven and which are judgment.
